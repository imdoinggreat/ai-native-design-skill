# Reusable design worksheets

Use only the worksheet relevant to the current stage. Keep records in the project's existing documentation location, or propose a compact location if none exists. These worksheets are optional working formats, not required new files.

## Project brief

- Purpose, audience, and primary user task
- Current implementation or designated source of truth
- Observed friction, with source
- Constraints: accessibility, devices, content, performance, stack, schedule
- References: what to borrow and why
- Anti-references: what to avoid and the positive alternative
- Prior decisions and their rationale
- Unknowns, explicit assumptions, and the uncertainty to reduce next

## Structural direction comparison

| Direction | Hypothesis | Layout and task path | Likely UX failure | Accessibility and mobile | Cost and maintenance | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use plain wireframes or route diagrams before visual decoration. Compare every direction against the same primary task. End with a recommendation, trade-offs, and a choice status: selected by the user, selected under delegated judgment, or awaiting selection.

## Validation plan and findings

- Decision being tested
- Participant profile and realistic task prompt
- Prototype state, device, and relevant limitations
- Observable success and failure signals, defined before the session
- Method: implementation check, expert review, or participant observation
- Observations and participant statements, separate from interpretations
- Open questions and confidence
- Earliest affected stage and next experiment

When no sessions have happened, fill in the plan and label findings as pending. Do not invent participants, quotations, completion rates, or outcomes. A small qualitative study can reveal confusion; it does not establish a population-wide effect.

For a metric, record its event or table source, time range and timezone, numerator, denominator, exclusions, attribution rule, transformations, and freshness. Mark any unavailable lineage explicitly. A drop in a funnel locates an issue; it does not establish its cause.

## Reusable learning record

- Observation and supporting evidence
- Candidate principle
- Scope: general technique or project preference
- Exceptions and confidence
- Unrelated ordinary task used to test transfer
- Transfer result and the revision it supports

Example: “Keep direct navigation beside optional camera exploration” may generalize to immersive interfaces. “Use low-poly materials” is a project preference unless broader evidence supports it.
